How to automatically reload Twitch player after an error

Nothing is more frustrating than leaving a Twitch stream in the background and realizing 30 minutes later that the player has crashed (Error 2000, 3000 or black screen). Here's how to automate video player reloading so you never experience interruptions again.

Updated on 11 August 2026

On this page

  • Why does the player stop?
  • How StreamPulse Auto-Refresh works
  • Activate it from the options

Why does the player stop?

You leave a stream in the background and suddenly the sound cuts out. Returning to the tab, you see a black screen with a "Click here to restart" button or a "Network Error (#2000)" message. This behavior occurs for several reasons:

  • Quality change: Twitch tries to adapt the resolution to your connection, which sometimes causes a bug in the HTML5 player.
  • WiFi micro-cuts: Loss of the network packet freezes the live stream.

How StreamPulse Auto-Refresh works

StreamPulse monitors the player constantly. As soon as an error code appears on the screen, it injects a command to reset the video player instantly.

The big advantage of this method compared to a classic refresh (F5) is that only the video stream is restarted. The chat remains active, you don't lose your message history and you don't have to reload all the heavy scripts on the page.

This is a must-have of the best Twitch extensions for those who keep multiple streams open at the same time.

Activate it from the options

Once StreamPulse is installed:

  • Open the extension dashboard (the icon in your Chrome bar).
  • Go to Settings (gear icon).
  • Check the Anti-Pause & Auto-Reload box.
